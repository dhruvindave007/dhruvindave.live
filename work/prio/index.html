The product film

Problem

In most task tools the work after a meeting is manual: someone has to remember what was decided, retype it into tickets, find the duplicates and chase the owners. Boards grow into backlogs nobody trusts, and managers spend their time grooming instead of deciding.

Prio is built around the manager’s four questions: what matters most right now, what is waiting on me, who is blocked, and who is overloaded. AI does the typing and people keep the decisions.

Work Intelligence: meetings into tasks

Everything is idempotent: the same meeting processed twice gives the same proposals, and re-applying one returns “already applied”. Today the extraction is rule-based and the model layer plugs into the same pipeline without changing the rest of the system. People are matched carefully and never guessed: a near-miss on a name goes to review instead of to the wrong person.

Text inside a transcript that looks like an instruction to the model is flagged and treated as data, so a meeting cannot talk the AI into changing anything.

Agents and MCP

Prio exposes a Model Context Protocol server, so AI assistants and internal agents can work with the task list directly. A personal token acts as that person and sees only their scope; a service token is read-only and fails closed unless it is given teams. Proposals from agents land in the same review inbox, and every AI write is recorded in the audit log.

Running the work

Team priorities gathers every task of every person a manager is responsible for into one board: three priority bands, drag to reorder, the top ten highlighted. Tasks have seven statuses, ordered steps with a handoff to the next owner, assignees, a reviewer and followers; Blocked always needs a note, and work marked Needs manager review lands in an Approvals list. Duplicates are caught before they are created, merges can be undone, and deleted tasks can be restored. Access is least-privilege: a manager edits only their own people’s work, with delegation while away. Team health shows workload so overload and silence show up before deadlines slip. Everyone gets a calendar, notifications and Ctrl+K search, and bulk import previews every row before saving. Signed sessions, hashed passwords, role checks on every request and an audit log are built in.

Why not Planner, Jira or Power Apps

Prio is not a Jira replacement, and it does not try to out-feature Jira or Azure DevOps Boards, which track development work well. Those tools are built to record tasks and leave the manager to dig for the signal. Prio makes different choices for a small team that also runs non-code work, such as partnerships, certifications and client asks, and wants less admin:

  • One ranked queue per teamManagers order the work; there is no sprawling backlog to groom. Employees cannot quietly change what they were asked to do, but they can propose tasks.
  • Meetings become tasksDecisions made in a meeting turn into reviewable task proposals with the quote that justifies them, instead of someone retyping notes into tickets.
  • AI that proposes, never actsEvery AI suggestion is idempotent, carries its evidence and waits for a human, so nothing changes behind a manager’s back.
  • Agents can work with itAn MCP server lets AI assistants read, propose and manage tasks within one person’s scope, with scoped, revocable, rate-limited tokens and every AI write in the audit log.
  • Calm by defaultNotifications arrive one quiet card at a time, and red means overdue on your own plate, not someone else’s.

Results

Before the model, a rules-only pipeline got 67% of actions right on a separate 21-case set of synthetic meetings, with zero hallucinated tasks or people. Meeting lines are analysed in parallel, which cut a four-line meeting from 70 s to 16 s, and dates are computed from the quoted words, because the model once turned “by Friday” into a Thursday.

The backend has about 730 tests against a throwaway Postgres, and CI also scans the frontend bundle for leaked secrets and checks every database migration. Releases go to staging first, then production.
